A software delivery office must decide whether adding two reviewers permanently will improve completion rates enough to justify the cost. One project team currently has four dedicated reviewers. An approved pilot will increase that allocation to six on a specified Monday and retain it for eight weeks, with reviewer responsibilities and approval criteria unchanged.

The office has twelve weeks of daily records covering requests submitted, reviews completed, unfinished work, item age, rework, and staff attendance. It can collect the same records throughout the pilot. Requests are classified by complexity, and the starting backlog includes both recent submissions and several unusually old items. Managers expect additional reviewers to help, but disagree about whether early gains would represent lasting capacity or simply the clearance of easy accumulated work.

The project director needs an evidence-based assessment of how quickly the team benefits, whether improvements persist, and whether the observed pattern supports a permanent staffing decision. The assessment must also inform commitments for a similar project beginning next quarter.

No additional hiring is possible during the pilot. Incoming requests cannot be suspended, reviewers need onboarding, and a planned product release may change the volume and complexity of submissions. Other teams share some specialists, so their availability is not fully under the director’s control.
