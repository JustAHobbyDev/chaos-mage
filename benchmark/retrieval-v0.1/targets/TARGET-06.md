An accounting team is reconstructing annual employer pension contributions for six subsidiaries before signing off on an acquisition’s historical liabilities. Each subsidiary retained an ordered ledger of additions to its cumulative contributions, but a software migration stripped calendar labels from several exports. Some exports may also omit an annual entry, repeat an entry, or assign a late adjustment to the wrong year.

The available evidence includes contribution amounts in their original order, opening and closing cumulative totals, and independently dated bank confirmations for two subsidiaries. The records cover different, partly overlapping stretches of roughly fifteen years. All six employers operated under the same national wage agreements and statutory contribution-rate changes, so their annual contributions were subject to several common increases, freezes, and reversals. Their absolute payment levels differ substantially.

The team must assign defensible accounting years to the undated entries, identify likely omissions or duplicates, and determine which portions of the reconstructed history remain too uncertain for audit reliance. Headcount changes, local bonuses, strikes, and payment deferrals may obscure common movements. Supporting payroll files are incomplete, some overlaps span only three years, and even dated reference records may contain posting errors. No reconstructed entry can be treated as a verified payment without corroborating evidence.
