A volunteer-run online community introduced an automated “trending discussions” panel three weeks ago. Since then, moderators have reported more hostile exchanges, particularly when older disagreements appear in the panel and attract new participants. The same period included a membership surge and a controversial industry announcement, so the panel’s role is unclear.

The operations team has hourly records of panel impressions and clicks, thread participation, user reports, moderator removals, and temporary account restrictions. Eight weeks of earlier activity are available for comparison. Reports sometimes arrive days after an exchange, and moderator coverage varies by weekday. Several arguments have continued through direct links even after their threads stopped appearing in the panel.

The team needs to decide whether to retain, modify, or retire the panel before the next monthly community meeting. Administrators can switch it off without affecting ordinary browsing, search, or subscriptions, and can restore the same configuration later. They cannot randomly assign individual members to different interfaces. A scheduled moderation-policy update will take effect in ten days, and a large public event is expected the following week.

Any operational changes must preserve access to discussions and avoid deliberately exposing members to harassment. Moderators can provide daily incident summaries, but they cannot substantially increase staffing. Community leaders want evidence that distinguishes the panel’s contribution from ordinary fluctuations and outside events, without treating a brief improvement or deterioration as decisive.
