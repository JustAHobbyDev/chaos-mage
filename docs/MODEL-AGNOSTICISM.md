# Model agnosticism — v0.1

Frozen before capability qualification measurements, 2026-09-29.

Chaos Mage is model-agnostic above a minimum reasoning and protocol-competence
threshold. The harness owns instrument representation, transfer-validity rules,
anti-collapse rules, schemas, native baselines, and experimental controls. Models
instantiate reasoning within that machinery.

Model agnosticism is not model interchangeability. Eligibility does not require
matching another model's labels, generations, novelty distribution, interpretations
of ambiguous cases, or a particular agreement percentage. Cross-model agreement
is evidence of robustness, not a prerequisite for eligibility.

## Eligibility

A research model must comply with frozen protocols and schemas, distinguish state,
operation, signal, inference, and limit, preserve operative mechanisms, reason about
evidential warrant and target fidelity, respect supplied native baselines, recognize
collapse without suppressing material modest departures, express bounded uncertainty,
and return inspectable, internally coherent structured rationales. The frozen
[capability contract](../distance/model-qualification-v0.1/CAPABILITY-CONTRACT.md)
operationalizes these requirements for the first qualification checkpoint.

## Permanent disagreement taxonomy

1. **Ontology / specification ambiguity:** the protocol or distinction permits
   multiple plausible interpretations. Examples include execution versus warrant
   conditions, whether a derived feasible set is a new observable, or whether a
   changed object makes an ordinary follow-up a new inquiry rule. This is a harness
   or research issue.
2. **Legitimate reasoning variation:** a coherent, defensible interpretation differs
   from another model under the same supplied information and frozen protocol.
   Examples include a different decisive departure locus or BORDERLINE_KEEP versus
   SUFFICIENT_DEPARTURE with the same keep decision.
3. **Model failure:** supplied constraints are ignored, the operative mechanism is
   lost, an inference lacks support, or structured output contradicts its rationale.
   Only this category directly argues against model eligibility.

A single debatable judgment is not a disqualifying pathology. Repeated schema
failures, unsupported inferences, baseline disregard, terminology-as-novelty,
formalization-as-departure, suppression of modest departures, conflation of
unobserved outcomes with invalid inference, or contradictory rationales can show
systematic failure. Qualification is a reasoned capability assessment, not voting
or an accuracy score against a historical model.

## Model provenance rule

Every experiment records exact requested and returned model identifiers (including
version/snapshot when exposed), provider endpoint, API/client version, reasoning
controls, sampling settings/defaults, tier, and non-secret credential provenance.
Unknown served snapshots remain explicitly unknown. Model agnosticism does not
erase provenance: historical experiments retain their original model identities
and interpretations. Later qualifications do not reinterpret historical results.

Historical Family A was OpenAI GPT-6 Astra; historical Family B was Anthropic Claude
Fable 5.1. Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor is a candidate for Family B, pending the
model-qualification-v0.1 decision. A successful qualification permits a documented
model-family transition, not a claim of equivalence. Future work must not casually
pool Fable and Muse agreement statistics. Experiment F remains unexecuted by this
checkpoint and may use Muse only following qualification and a separate handoff.
